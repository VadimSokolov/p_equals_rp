# Chapter 31. Lorenz Curves, Majorization and Concentration

The Lorenz identity Z = integral of the quantile function Lambda over (0, 1) is the hinge of the book, and this chapter sets out the theory of Lorenz curves behind it. It recalls Lorenz's 1905 curve and Gini's 1912 coefficient, relates the book's richest first curve to Lorenz's poorest first curve, and presents majorization with the Hardy, Littlewood and Polya theorem that majorization is equivalent to action of a doubly stochastic matrix and to inequalities for convex functions. It reads simulated annealing as a path in the majorization order from the uniform distribution to the ground states. It then shows the rearrangement invariance of the vertical approach (the quantile function determines the walk, the geometry of the space only enters through the level set samplers), introduces the concentration function of Cifarelli and Regazzini, which is the Lorenz curve of a likelihood ratio and links to the Neyman and Pearson lemma, describes the product estimator as a composition of concentration functions with a kink at rho, and proves a deterministic bracket of order 1/n for Riemann sums of a monotone quantile function. No new theorem is catalogued.

## From the literature

- Lorenz curve (Lorenz 1905) and Gini coefficient (Gini 1912)
- Majorization and the theorem that a is majorized by b if and only if a = Mb with M doubly stochastic (Hardy, Littlewood and Polya 1929)
- Concentration function (Cifarelli and Regazzini 1987)
- Neyman and Pearson lemma
