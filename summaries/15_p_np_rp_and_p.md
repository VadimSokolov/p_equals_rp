This chapter sets out the complexity classes needed for the thesis and the known relations among them. It defines P, NP (with the Cook Levin theorem and the queens completion problem as an NP complete example), the counting class #P (Valiant 1979, Toda's theorem, and the convention that the sensible queens counting object is the completion count), and the randomized classes RP, coRP, ZPP and BPP with amplification. It states the inclusions P in ZPP in RP in NP and BPP, the canonical problem polynomial identity testing, and the facts known about P versus RP: Adleman's RP in P/poly, the Sipser, Gács and Lautemann containment of BPP in the second level of the hierarchy, the Valiant Vazirani isolation theorem, the hardness versus randomness theorems (Nisan and Wigderson, Impagliazzo and Wigderson: if E needs exponential circuits then P = BPP), the Kabanets and Impagliazzo barrier, and the hitting set generator formulation (Andreev, Clementi and Rolim; Goldreich, Vadhan and Wigderson). It ends with three worlds for randomness and says which one the thesis addresses.
