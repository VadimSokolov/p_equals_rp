This chapter covers the results that escape the relativization and natural proofs barriers by arithmetization, and what they mean for the program. It describes interactive proofs (Goldwasser, Micali and Rackoff; Babai), Shamir's theorem IP = PSPACE built on the sum check protocol of Lund, Fortnow, Karloff and Nisan, and the PCP theorem (Arora, Safra, Lund, Motwani, Sudan and Szegedy; Dinur's gap amplification proof) with its equivalence to hardness of approximating MAX 3SAT. It observes that hardness versus randomness relativizes, so a proof of P = RP through a hitting set from a hard function reduces to a circuit lower bound that algebrizing techniques cannot prove (Aaronson and Wigderson). It then speculates, explicitly as suggestive rather than established, that PCP encodings correspond to an energy with a gap, and asks what would fail on hard boards: shattering and torpid mixing of the level set chain versus an empty level. No new theorem is stated.

## From the literature
