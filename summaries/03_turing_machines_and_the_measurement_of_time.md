This chapter gives the formal definitions on which the complexity classes rest. It defines the deterministic Turing machine, the universal machine, the halting problem and Rice's theorem, and then running time, with the classes DTIME, P, E and EXP and the space classes L and PSPACE. It states and sketches the time hierarchy theorem (more time buys more languages) and notes that its diagonal proof relativizes. It explains why polynomial time is the standard notion of efficiency (closure under composition, invariance across models, and the empirical record with its exceptions such as the ellipsoid method and the simplex method), discusses worst case versus average case time, and introduces Boolean circuits and the class P/poly, including the layout of a computation as a circuit that underlies the Cook Levin theorem. It contains no new results and fixes the conventions for later chapters.
