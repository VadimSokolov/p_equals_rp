# Chapter 9. What a Resolution Would Mean

This chapter describes the worlds in which P = NP or P is not NP and what each would mean for mathematics, cryptography, optimisation and for the Monte Carlo program. If P = NP the polynomial hierarchy collapses, optimisation and proof search become feasible, and cryptography based on one way functions fails; Levin's universal search shows constructiveness is not the issue. If P is not NP the picture is as it appears, with the status of cryptography depending on one way functions. The chapter then describes intermediate worlds in Impagliazzo's classification (Algorithmica, Heuristica, Pessiland, Minicrypt, Cryptomania), the possibilities NP in BPP, and NP in P/poly (Karp and Lipton), and the role of counting through Toda's theorem. It states what the program means in each world: trivial in Algorithmica, succeeding on random but not constructed boards in Heuristica, and so on. No new theorem is stated.
