This chapter records the contributions of John von Neumann and Claude Shannon on which the Monte Carlo program rests. For von Neumann it covers the stored program architecture (the EDVAC report, 1945), his work on reliable computation from unreliable components (1956), the origin of the Monte Carlo method with Ulam and the letter to Richtmyer (1947), the rejection method and the extraction of fair bits from a biased coin (1951), his remark on arithmetical production of random digits, and the random walk method for inverting matrices of von Neumann and Ulam as reported by Forsythe and Leibler. For Shannon it covers entropy and channel capacity (1948) and the random coding argument, the counting argument for circuits (1949), secrecy and the one time pad, and the chess paper and Shannon number (1950). It connects entropy to counting queens solutions, and the vertical ladder to staged rejection sampling. It is historical and contains no new theorem.
