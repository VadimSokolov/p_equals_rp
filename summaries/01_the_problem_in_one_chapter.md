This opening chapter states the P versus NP problem and surveys the whole book in the form of a long essay. It explains the difference between problems that are easy to solve and problems that are easy to check, defines the classes P, NP, RP, BPP and #P informally, and tells the history from Hilbert's Entscheidungsproblem through Gödel's letter of 1956 to the Millennium Prize Problem. It lists what is known, namely the three barriers (relativization, natural proofs, algebrization), the consequences of P = NP, Ladner's theorem and the hardness versus randomness theorems that make P = BPP believable. It then introduces the book's laboratory, the N queens completion problem, a #P complete counting problem with an energy given by the number of attacking pairs, and the thesis that counting and sampling can be seen through the quantile function of a level set walk (vertical likelihood Monte Carlo). The chapter summarises the findings of the later parts (connectivity of level sets, mixing of the swap chain, density of states, cross entropy and cloning methods) without proving anything new. It is a reading guide: the sampling half (NP = RP) has computations and theorems, while the derandomization half (P = RP) restates the known hitting set formulation.
