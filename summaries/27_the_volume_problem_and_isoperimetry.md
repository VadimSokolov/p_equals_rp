# Chapter 27. The Volume Problem and Isoperimetry

The volume of a convex body is a sharp P hard problem with a complete theory, and the chapter presents it as the model for what a mixing proof for the queens would look like. It states the lower bound that no deterministic polynomial time algorithm in the oracle model approximates the volume within an exponential factor (Elekes, Barany and Furedi), the randomized polynomial time algorithm of Dyer, Frieze and Kannan built from a ladder of bodies and a random walk controlled by an isoperimetric inequality, and the later improvements of the exponent through Lovasz and Vempala and Gaussian cooling of Cousins and Vempala. It extends the analysis to log concave densities (Applegate and Kannan; Frieze, Kannan and Polson) and reports the Kannan, Lovasz and Simonovits conjecture on the isoperimetric constant, with the reductions and bounds of Eldan, Lee and Vempala, Chen, Klartag and Lehec. It ends with what distinguishes volume from the queens: convexity supplies isoperimetry, and the queens level sets have no convexity. No new theorem is stated.

## From the literature

- Impossibility of deterministic volume approximation in the oracle model (Elekes 1986; Barany and Furedi 1987)
- Randomized polynomial time volume algorithm (Dyer, Frieze and Kannan 1991)
- Faster algorithms (Lovasz and Vempala 2006); Gaussian cooling (Cousins and Vempala 2018)
- Sampling log concave functions (Applegate and Kannan 1991; Frieze, Kannan and Polson 1994)
- Kannan, Lovasz and Simonovits conjecture (1995) and its progress (Eldan 2013; Lee and Vempala 2017; Chen 2021; Klartag and Lehec 2022; Klartag 2023)
